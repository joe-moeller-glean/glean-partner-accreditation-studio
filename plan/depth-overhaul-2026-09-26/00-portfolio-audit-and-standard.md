This is a research-and-build plan, not a claim that the courses have been updated or released. The spreadsheet was read directly through Glean; its last reported update was 11 September 2026. Tracker IDs and statuses match the repository's 83-row `plan/queue.json` snapshot. ## How to use the plan

Start with the file-by-file local audit (08) and this standard, then four tracker-module volumes (01–04), the capstone/accreditation volume (05), the local-extra/gap-fill volume (06), and the parallel execution contract (07). Each module entry gives **research questions; the sequence of explanations to write; a specific diagram or capture; a practical performance task; and a neighboring-course change**. The existing 83 `plan/modules/<ID>.md` files are prior drafts, not a production-ready specification: their screen-copy caps and nine-screen formula conflict with the depth standard below. Do not regenerate their thin layout under a new theme. The existing custom-connector plan is a useful deeper proposed branch; reconcile it in volume 05 rather than replacing it.

### Confirmed substantive weaknesses and high-risk contradictions

1. The old `plan/01-design-system.md` caps many A3 explanation screens at ≤90 words; the module specs repeat a nearly identical Hook → Model → Sort → Quiz → Job aid spine. This is a **design constraint causing superficiality**, not merely a missing illustration problem. Remove instructional word caps and enforce a learning-content standard instead.
2. The local `BLD-207` blueprint calls its assessment a *Lab* but its `assessment/items.json` has five single-select questions. `ACC-104` is a five-question quiz even though the tracker describes a Builder accreditation involving agents, actions, and connectors. `SRV-204` is also a lab-shaped topic with a five-question assessment. These are explicit assessment-type mismatches; the capstone descriptions in volume 05 supersede them.
3. The existing Agents course cluster drew product-behavior claims from older partner decks. The memory recommendation from `AGT-101` records four conflicts with newer product docs: trigger taxonomy, Auto vs Workflow mode, the five building blocks, and the agent development lifecycle. Reverify every copied claim in FND-104/105, BLD-101–104, SRV-204, BLD-207/208, SOL-203, and ACC-104 before reusing copy. [cite: How agents work](https://docs.glean.com/agents/how-agents-work)
4. Sources about program mechanics are not product-behavior authority. Current connector documentation distinguishes indexed, live, and hybrid access and the role of source permissions. An MCP/live tool does not by itself make all source content searchable. Rework any diagram or exercise that blends these. 5. Agent competency, connector competency, and embedded-experiences competency are **organization-level external audits**, not automatically conferred by an LMS quiz. ## What expert-level learning means here

**Minimum learning design, per topic:** (1) an observable task and required prior knowledge; (2) a 500–1,500-word *appropriate-to-scope* clear explanation with mechanisms, preconditions, nonexamples, edge cases, and what is unknown; (3) one claim-backed annotated system diagram plus a sequential caption/text equivalent; (4) at least one real redacted, dated product UI capture for each UI-dependent procedure, with 3–8 numbered callouts and a text procedure; (5) a fully worked customer example with inputs, decisions, outputs, and trade-offs; (6) a guided practice with explanatory feedback; (7) a transfer task on a different case; (8) a carry-away job aid/template; (9) assessment evidence aligned to the actual badge promise. These are design planning ranges, **not** filler-word targets. A topic that cannot support them should be narrower or combined, not padded.

**Time and rigor, not an arbitrary countdown:** Rebaseline the 0.5–1.5-hour tracker targets in pilot testing. A foundation concept may need 60–90 minutes; a technical/design course 90–150 minutes; a real lab 2–4 hours; a capstone 2–5 hours plus review. Show estimated active learning, lab, and assessment times separately. Ask the roadmap owner to approve changes to `Duration Hours`, `Assessment`, or badge policy. Never hold a learner on a screen or gate only by dwell time; require demonstrated mastery and honest progress. Provide an optional deep-dive appendix for prerequisites and product variance rather than cramming it into a 30-minute entry course.

**Three distinct modalities:** seller courses use transcript analysis, discovery calls, pitch/demo rehearsals, qualification and objections; technical courses use data-flow tracing, permissions tests, configuration and incident diagnosis; services courses use workshops, readiness gates, RACIs, rollout plans, adoption/value measurement, and escalation artifacts. Cross-path cases reuse a fictional organization but change constraints and decision criteria. A course must have a unique final artifact and misconception set. Do not copy a generic 80%-on-five-MCQ contract to every path.

**Assessment floor proposed for review:** 100-level concept courses: 8–12 mixed application/decision items plus one unscored rehearsal; 200-level: 10–15 mixed items or two distinct scenarios with scored artifact; 300-level: authentic lab with reproducible test evidence and a graded rubric. Accreditation: at least two unseen integrated cases, a role-specific artifact/performance task, and an independent scored review; an item bank may be supplemental. Set 80% only as a proposal pending policy approval; hard gates (permissions, factual accuracy, unsafe actions, missing customer handoff) cannot be offset by a high aggregate score. Use alternate case sets for retries and targeted remediation. A quiz in an offline SCORM package is not secure high-stakes proof; keep assessor-graded work outside the package, track LMS completion separately, and do not include authoring answer keys in runtime ZIPs.

## Research and production protocol for **every** module

1. **Intake.** Read its tracker row (`Description`, audience, format, duration, mapped asset, source basis, prerequisite, owner, notes), current local files if any, old plan, sibling blueprints, `memory/` decisions/rejections/recommendations, and the current badge YAML. Record whether an actual source asset is obtained or merely referenced. Confirm audience and one tangible performance outcome.
2. **Source discovery.** Use Glean enterprise search for internal program/decks/delivery playbooks and current Glean product documentation for behavior; follow Developer docs for APIs/SDK; use a named owner for any partner policy or support SLA. Add at least two independent authoritative sources, or one designated system of record plus owner sign-off. Current docs supersede old decks for product behavior. Assign `source_id`, exact URL, title, owner, updated and verified dates, relevant passage, permission to reuse/capture, 90-day review for product/UI/security/pricing or 180-day for program policy; flag conflicts and unsupported assertions.
3. **Mechanism investigation.** Ask what happens before, during, and after the task; what data, identities, permissions, network/API requests, approvals, and human handoffs exist; what fails and how the user diagnoses it; what differs by connector, deployment, plan, role, region, or beta. Verify each step and exact UI label against current docs or a controlled test tenant. If docs do not establish a behavior, use a clear conditional or cut it.
4. **Expert interview and asset clearance.** Ask the tracker owner/SME for two real anonymized failure cases, one successful implementation, current limits, exceptions, and audit criteria. Request approved product screenshots/tenant access, redaction approval, and logo/media rights. If unavailable, use an accurate annotated HTML/SVG state model instead of fabricated UI; keep the screenshot need open.
5. **Learning architecture.** Map every outcome → explanation section → worked example → practice → assessment criterion. Write an executable case packet: clearly synthetic, unnamed organization profile, source/app inventory, permission matrix, workflow constraints, stakeholders, baseline measures, artifacts, and answer/rubric. Differentiate breadth from mastery: introductory lesson explains; intermediate lesson chooses; advanced lab proves and handles failure.
6. **Write full explanations.** Use overview → causal model → numbered procedure → why each step matters → safe/unsafe example → failure/variant → decision rule. Define technical terms at first use. Put only claim-verified behavior into learner copy; attach `claim_id` to every factual statement. Include an expandable deep dive and glossary rather than compressing causal explanation to fit a card.
7. **Diagram/image production.** For each meaningful diagram, write a diagram specification first: learner question, data flow, entities, trust boundaries, direction, error/permission path, sources/claim IDs, callouts, alt text, a long text equivalent, review owner, and version. Reuse a shared skeleton only when the semantics are identical; alter content/layout to answer the module's specific question. Use a flow with states for a process, sequence for cross-system calls, swimlane for ownership, topology for networking, comparison matrix for choices, and causal tree for troubleshooting. Images must illuminate a real concept; avoid decorative SVG count as a success metric. Screenshots: real, dated, redacted, local/offline, numbered, captioned, and reverified after UI change. Never generate imitation product UI.
8. **Build and test.** Parallel authors can produce copy, assessment/case packet, and diagrams from the same locked claims and blueprint; integrate only after each independent review. Run fact/editorial, accessibility, brand, package hygiene, SCORM, keyboard/assistive-tech, responsive, offline, rubric inter-rater, and live LMS import/restore/score tests. Bind QA to the exact ZIP SHA and exact source commit. No sub-agent approves its own output.
9. **Refresh and publish.** On a changed behavior claim, search every consumer by `claim_id`, update the diagram/capture and question bank, rerun regressions, record a versioned changelog and ledger rationale, and update the tracker **only after** authorized publication. Weekly drift scan, monthly source/UI freshness audit, and quarterly item analytics are proposed operations; this plan does not create automations or publish anything.
