# SBP-111 Blueprint Deliverables, MAP and Handoff

## SBP-111-L01 Assemble the Blueprint deliverable set
@src S05 S13 S14 S45

A Blueprint engagement produces a connected set of artifacts. Together they let someone who wasn't in the room understand the priorities, the evidence, the decisions, and what happens next.

### The complete pack
This course proposes a standard pack, assembled from Glean's Blueprint and value materials:
1. **The value hypothesis**, in the customer's language.
2. **The stakeholder map:** sponsor, economic buyer, champion, technical owners, and workflow owners.
3. **The prioritized use-case log and matrix**, with scores and the rationale for each.
4. **DITL maps:** the current and future state for each priority workflow.
5. **Metric cards**, with baselines, sources, and owners.
6. **The value estimate:** ranges, assumptions, and evidence labels.
7. **Executive decisions:** what was accepted, conditional, or deferred.
8. **The validation recommendation.**
9. **The mutual action plan (MAP).**

Include the assumptions and a version history. Glean sources don't all require the same set of files or the same layout. Treat this as a proposed standard, not a mandate.

### Make every priority traceable
@src S13
@quote S13 | Pre-sales value hypotheses are often lost after the deal closes.
Glean's Value Playbook names a common failure: pre-sales value hypotheses are often lost after the deal closes. Avoid it by making each priority traceable from the business outcome, through the workflow change, to a measure and an accountable owner. The executive summary should link to the evidence, not repeat every detail.

### At a glance
The pack should let a delivery team or customer success manager pick up the account without re-running discovery.

Figure: flow | How the Blueprint pack builds | A five-step build of the Blueprint deliverable set: the value hypothesis and stakeholder map, the prioritized use case log and matrix, DITL maps and metric cards, the value estimate and executive decisions, and the validation recommendation with the mutual action plan.
- Hypothesis and stakeholders :: Why change, and who decides
- Use case log and matrix :: Agreed priorities
- DITL maps and metric cards :: How the work happens; how value is measured
- Value and decisions :: Ranges and executive commitments
- *Validation and MAP :: What happens next, and who owns it

### Practice
Audit a pack that has an excellent ROI model but no workflow records and no named owners.

### Worked response
The pack is incomplete. Add the prioritized log and matrix, the current and future workflow maps, named business and technical owners, metric and evidence cards, the decision log, the validation plan, and the MAP. A financial model on its own is not a handoff.

## SBP-111-L02 Build a Mutual Action Plan
@src S45 S15 S14

A mutual action plan (MAP) is the joint sequence of commitments, from both the customer and the Glean and partner team, that leads from the next validation to a decision and beyond.

### What each entry records
@src S45
Glean's MAP template lists the evaluation committee (executive sponsor, economic buyer, champion, procurement contact, and implementation contacts), restates the problem and the agreed measures of success, and then sets out dated activities, each with an owner and a purpose. Every MAP entry should capture:
- **The milestone**, as a concrete event.
- **The owner**, on the customer or Glean and partner side.
- **Dependencies.**
- **The target date**, accepted by the owner.
- **Acceptance evidence:** how everyone will know it's done.
- **Status.**

### Make "do the pilot" specific
Instead of "do the pilot," list:
1. Readiness and approval.
2. Kickoff.
3. The scoped tests.
4. The findings review.
5. The executive decision.
6. The next commercial or delivery milestone, such as security review, order form, or onboarding.

For a demo or Lab validation, use a smaller plan with the same shape.

> **Boundary:** A seller's private close plan isn't automatically mutual. The champion and the relevant decision-makers must confirm their own commitments. Never invent dates on their behalf.

### At a glance
Every MAP entry answers the same six questions. If one is blank, the entry isn't a commitment yet.

Figure: cards | Anatomy of a MAP entry | Six cards naming the fields of a mutual action plan entry: the milestone, the owner, dependencies, the target date, acceptance evidence, and status.
- Milestone :: A specific, observable step
- Owner :: One named person
- Dependencies :: What must happen first
- Target date :: Agreed, not assumed
- *Acceptance evidence :: How everyone will know it's done
- Status :: Kept current after every meeting

### Practice
Turn "technical validation next month" into measurable MAP entries.

### Worked response
1. **Confirm scope and readiness.** Owners: the SE and the customer's IT lead. Target: [date]. Evidence: a signed-off scope note.
2. **Run the agreed validation event.** Owners: the SE and the champion. Target: [date]. Evidence: completed test tasks.
3. **Review findings against the criteria.** Owners: the joint team. Target: [date].
4. **Record the sponsor's decision.** Owner: the sponsor. Target: [date].
5. **Schedule the next gate.** Owner: the AE.

Ask each customer owner to accept their dates.

## SBP-111-L03 Recap and decision documentation
@src S05 S14 S46

Recaps and decision logs are the record of what was actually agreed. Write them precisely, and keep internal judgments separate.

### Two kinds of record
- **Customer-facing recap:** purpose, priorities, evidence, decisions, open items, and actions.
- **Internal record:** qualification judgments, scores, and restricted process information. Glean's opportunity workbook is an example of an internal record.

Keep versions and ownership clear for every artifact.

### The decision log
Table: Decision log statuses
| Status | Meaning | Record |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted | Agreed as stated | Rationale and approver |
| Conditional | Agreed, subject to a named condition | The condition, its owner, and the date |
| Deferred | Postponed, not rejected | The reason and the revisit date |
| Rejected | Not pursued | The rationale |

When the scope is revised, point to the version used in the value model and the validation criteria. This keeps the numbers and the scope consistent.

> **Boundary:** Don't overstate tentative agreement or expose internal scores. Verify recipients and access permissions before sharing. A draft does not authorize sending.

### At a glance
The customer recap and the internal record serve different readers. Keep both, and keep them consistent.

Figure: compare | Two kinds of record | A comparison of the customer-facing recap and the internal record. The recap confirms decisions, commitments, and next steps in customer language. The internal record adds evidence levels, risks, and open questions for the account team.
- Customer-facing recap || Internal record
- Decisions and commitments, in their words || Evidence level behind each decision
- Next steps with owners and dates || Risks, gaps, and open questions
- Sent within a day || Updated in the opportunity and MAP

### Practice
Rewrite this recap line: "Everyone loved it and we're ready for rollout."

### Worked response
"The team prioritized three workflows. The sponsor accepted the outcome hypothesis, subject to two validations: confirming access behavior for restricted content, and establishing the handle-time baseline. [Owner names] will resolve these before the go or no-go checkpoint on [date]."

## SBP-111-L04 Attach scoped partner services
@src S43 S44 S05

Partners add the most value when their services address what the customer's roadmap actually needs. Every service should have a clear causal link to a customer outcome.

### Why partner-led services
@src S43
@quote S43 | Partners own outcome delivery, rollout enablement, and change management.
Glean's partner-led services model has partners owning outcome delivery, rollout enablement, and change management. Glean retains governance, deployment quality, and technical authority.

### Map services to needs
Map the prioritized roadmap to the services it requires:
- **Discovery facilitation**, to expand the Blueprint to new teams.
- **Deployment and connectors.**
- **Agent design and implementation.**
- **Adoption and change management.**
- **Governance and knowledge readiness.**
- **Value measurement and optimization.**

Define the outcomes, deliverables, responsibilities, assumptions, and exclusions in a scope that has been reviewed.

### Scope is a framework, not a promise of everything
@src S44
@quote S44 | The package level is a sizing and delivery framework; it is not a commitment to connect all possible data sources or deliver every identified use case.
Glean's partner SOW template states that a package level is a sizing and delivery framework, not a commitment to connect every possible data source or deliver every identified use case. Discovery outputs don't authorize delivery work or set contractual terms.

> **Boundary:** Don't attach a generic services SKU unless it clearly causes the customer outcome.

### At a glance
Map each service to a need found in the Blueprint, so scope follows evidence.

Figure: cards | Partner services mapped to Blueprint needs | Six cards mapping partner services to the needs they address: discovery facilitation, deployment and connectors, agent design and implementation, adoption and change management, governance and knowledge readiness, and value measurement and optimization.
- Discovery facilitation :: More Visioning or DITL work
- Deployment and connectors :: Getting the data in
- Agent design :: Building the priority workflows
- Adoption and change :: Getting people to use it
- Knowledge readiness :: Governance and content quality
- *Value measurement :: Proving the outcome

### Practice
Propose a services sequence for a priority workflow with poor content quality and low adoption readiness.

### Worked response
1. **Readiness and content ownership:** clean up the source content and assign owners.
2. **Scoped solution and configuration** for the workflow.
3. **Training and adoption**, with team leads.
4. **Value measurement and optimization** against the agreed baseline.

Confirm each phase's scope and owner, and get it reviewed, before any commitment.

## SBP-111-L05 Handoff to delivery and customer success
@src S13 S44 S28

A good handoff means delivery and customer success don't have to restart discovery. The receiving owners also get what they need to prove value later.

### What to hand off
- The business problem, the sponsor, and the champion.
- The prioritized workflows, with current and future maps.
- Data sources and permission considerations.
- Value assumptions and success measures.
- Validation findings.
- The scope, including exclusions, unverified capabilities, and customer obligations.
- The MAP.

### What customer success needs from the value model
@src S13
@quote S13 | The number one measurement failure is launching without a baseline.
Glean's Value Playbook calls launching without a baseline the number one measurement failure. Customer success and value owners need:
- Metric definitions and the cohort.
- The baseline period, or the plan to create a baseline.
- Targets and data owners.
- Evidence labels, plus the recapture and reallocation assumptions.
- Costs and attribution caveats.
- A review cadence and a named, accountable value owner.

### Acceptance
The receiving owner must accept the handoff explicitly. Record any unresolved dependencies and risks.

> **Boundary:** Don't hand over an aspirational future-state diagram as an implementation commitment.

### At a glance
Customer success inherits the value model. Hand it over with its assumptions, not just its headline.

Figure: flow | Value handoff to customer success | A four-step handoff of the value model: share the metric cards and baselines, explain the assumptions and evidence levels, agree the measurement cadence, and confirm acceptance by the delivery and customer success owners.
- Metric cards and baselines :: What will be tracked
- Assumptions and evidence :: What still needs proof
- Measurement cadence :: When results are reviewed
- *Acceptance :: Delivery and CS confirm they own it

### Practice
List what customer success needs from a pre-sales ROI estimate.

### Worked response
Metric definitions, the baseline period, the cohort, targets, data owners, evidence labels, the recapture and reallocation assumptions, costs, attribution caveats, a review cadence, and a named, accountable value owner who accepts the handoff.

## SBP-111-L06 Measure and expand responsibly
@src S13 S28 S05

After go-live, compare what was projected with what is emerging. Let observed outcomes, not the best-case pre-sales scenario, guide expansion.

### The post-go-live review
@src S13
@quote S13 | This playbook creates explicit handoff protocols so post-sales teams can validate what pre-sales promised.
A review at around 60 or 90 days can inspect:
- **Adoption:** who is using the workflow, and how often.
- **Workflow quality:** whether answers are correct and trusted.
- **System-of-record outcomes:** whether the business metric moved.
- **Reallocation:** whether capacity was deliberately redeployed.

Then decide: remediate, scale the current use case, or move on to the next strategic priority.

### Usage is not value
Early activity is not full financial ROI. Timing, evidence, and attribution determine what can be claimed. Before using a customer result in an external story, confirm the customer's permission and refresh the sources.

### Measure three ways
@src S72
Glean's example business value analysis describes three complementary measurement methods. User feedback comes from short surveys before and after, and across an evaluation. Product telemetry gives concrete usage data, including at a feature level. Stakeholder interviews give a deeper understanding of existing bottlenecks and where time saved is actually recaptured. Telemetry shows use. Only the interviews and the business's own systems show whether the outcome moved.

Figure: cards | Three ways to measure value | Three cards naming complementary measurement methods: user feedback through short pre- and post-surveys, product telemetry for concrete usage data, and stakeholder interviews to learn where saved time is actually recaptured.
- User feedback :: Short pre- and post-surveys
- Product telemetry :: Usage, including at a feature level
- *Stakeholder interviews :: Where saved time is actually recaptured

### Practice
Adoption is rising, but the business KPI hasn't moved. What should happen?

### Worked response
Investigate before claiming value. Check:
- Workflow fit: does the new flow match how the work is done?
- Data coverage and answer quality.
- Measurement lag.
- Whether the freed capacity was actually reallocated.

Then update the hypothesis and the plan. Don't treat usage as proof of realized financial value.
