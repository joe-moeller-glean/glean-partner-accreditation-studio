## 1. Narrative frame

- **Recurring persona:** Dana, with Ravi (delivery lead) as the reality check
- **Through-line situation:** Eleven candidate use cases and a pilot that can carry three
- **Cold open (A1) hook:** Dana's shortlist has three use cases. Ravi says two of them cannot be delivered in the pilot window. Who is right?
- **Signature moment (one T3 reveal):** Prioritization matrix resolves into a sequenced pilot plan with a visible cut line

## 2. Learning outcomes (observable)

- `OBJ-01` — Score candidate use cases on value, feasibility, data readiness, and time to value
- `OBJ-02` — Apply a cut line and defend what was deprioritized
- `OBJ-03` — Sequence a pilot so each use case reduces risk for the next
- `OBJ-04` — Validate a prioritized shortlist against delivery capacity

## 5. Interactions

- **`INT-MATRIX`** — Score six use cases across four dimensions with numeric selects; totals drive the cut line.
- **`INT-FADE`** — Worked scoring before independent scoring.
- **`INT-SEQ`** — Sequence the surviving use cases for risk reduction.
- **`INT-BRANCH`** — Deprioritization defense and a delivery-capacity challenge.
- Keyboard-equivalent path required in every component; state persisted to `cmi.suspend_data`; restores exactly on resume.

## 6. Assessment plan

- **Format:** Facilitator review, rubric-scored (tracker: `Facilitator review`)
- **Blueprint:** Learner facilitates or documents a prioritization session: scored shortlist, explicit cut line with rationale, sequence with dependency logic, and a capacity check.
- **Mastery:** 80%, retries reshuffled, explanatory feedback on final attempt
- **Distractor sources:** Embedded checks target: scoring on value alone, ignoring data readiness, sequencing without dependencies, ignoring delivery capacity.

## 7. Job aid / carry-away

- Prioritization worksheet with the scoring model, cut-line rationale template, and capacity check.
